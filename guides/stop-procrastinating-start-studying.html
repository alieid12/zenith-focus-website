How to Stop Procrastinating and Start Studying: A 10-Minute Reset

When you keep postponing a study session, the problem can feel bigger every time you think about it. Instead of waiting until you feel motivated, make the first step small enough to do now.

1. Name one task—not an entire subject

Replace “study chemistry” with something visible and finishable, such as “review two pages of notes” or “solve three practice questions.” A clear task removes the guesswork about where to begin.

2. Make starting take less than two minutes

Open the right book or document, put the materials you need on your desk, and write the first action. Do not reorganise your whole workspace or build a perfect timetable before you start.

3. Put one distraction out of reach

Silence non-essential notifications, close unrelated tabs, and place your phone somewhere inconvenient. If your phone contains course materials, prepare what you need first and use only that material during the session.

4. Commit to ten minutes

Set a ten-minute timer and work only on the task you chose. You are allowed to stop when the timer ends. The goal is to practise beginning, not to force yourself into a marathon.

5. Decide what happens after the timer

When the ten minutes are over, choose deliberately: continue for another short block, take a real break, or write down the next step for later. Avoid drifting automatically into social media.

6. Make the next session easier

Before you finish, leave your notes open to the next page, write one specific next action, and choose when you will return. A prepared starting point reduces friction tomorrow.

Your 10-minute reset

  1. Minute 1: choose one small task.
  2. Minutes 2–3: prepare the materials and remove one distraction.
  3. Minutes 4–10: work on the task without switching.
